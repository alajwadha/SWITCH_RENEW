# Productive uses: what the literature supports

Review date: 28 September 2026. This contribution includes **18 publications or official survey records**. It is a critical scoping review, not proof of exhaustive coverage. Two items were reviewed from publisher abstracts; one is a clearly labeled preprint. Publication and fieldwork years remain separate in the accompanying records.

**Somalia already has productive-energy studies, field evaluations and sector surveys. The defensible research opportunity is to connect verified energy use with reliable services, business outcomes and sustainable water use.** Another generic “first solar feasibility study” would require a much narrower, carefully checked novelty claim.

## Evidence across uses

| Use | Existing evidence | What it can support | What remains unresolved |
|---|---|---|---|
| Irrigation and farm production | Pump-design simulation, field-program evidence, national time-series analysis, small control prototype: PU03, PU11, PU12, PU17 | Technology design and implementation hypotheses | Seasonal energy/water measurements; causal technology effects; sustainable abstraction |
| Drinking water and desalination | Hydrogeology, project evaluation, investment scenarios and a coastal simulation: PU02, PU05, PU13, PU14 | Resource constraints, service costing and maintenance research | Validated local costs, corrected desalination assumptions and actual service continuity |
| Fisheries and milk | Organizational survey, market baseline and centre inspections: PU06, PU07, PU15 | Where equipment, transport, ownership and finance interact | Refrigeration duty, spoilage, temperature compliance, downtime and distribution of benefits |
| Commerce and industry | Formal-enterprise survey: PU10 | Weighted analysis of eligible firms’ electricity use | Informal/microenterprise coverage, hourly demand and meter validation |
| Port infrastructure | Hobyo hybrid-system simulation: PU18 | Port-demand and storage research questions | Load provenance, local reservoir feasibility and audited costs |
| Health and education | Hospital simulation, health costing and school planning: PU01, PU08, PU09 | Facility/load data leads and investment pipelines | Commissioning, critical-load reliability, maintenance and service outcomes |

## Interpretation and checks

The [hospital study (PU01)](https://acikerisim.gelisim.edu.tr/server/api/core/bitstreams/87c19563-85cd-4b1d-a3d9-ba18eb9ec33e/content) provides a particularly useful lead to historical measured load. Its simulated configuration should remain separate from evidence about actual reliability. The [irrigation chapter (PU03)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-62881-8_1) is conditional design evidence; obtaining its head and duty assumptions matters more than copying a headline cost. The [desalination article (PU02)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10098-024-02741-1) has a publisher-linked [correction](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10098-024-02797-z) whose body was inaccessible. Its numerical findings should remain provisional.

Operational research adds what design models miss. [UNICEF’s final evaluation (PU13)](https://evaluationreports.unicef.org/GetDocument?documentID=8933&fileID=35944) connects pumping with procurement, installation and maintenance. The [water-investment brief (PU14)](https://www.unicef.org/somalia/media/1241/file/Somalia-sustainable-water-supply-options-2019.pdf) compares mixed service strategies, so it cannot isolate a solar return. The [groundwater assessment (PU05)](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099615012012129914/pdf/P1749940c70d5d09008a3203ad9eeb97d1a.pdf) supplies physical constraints that energy optimization must respect.

Agricultural outcomes also need careful attribution. [Datta’s program synthesis (PU12)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00307270251364907) concerns combined interventions. [Nor’s national analysis (PU11)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23311932.2026.2616845) estimates aggregate associations, not farm-level solar effects. The [Mogadishu irrigation prototype (PU17)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23311932.2026.2696549) tests control of water use at a very small scale; transferring its result to energy savings requires additional measurement. [Kassa and colleagues (PU16)](https://eartharxiv.org/repository/view/14054/) add stakeholder questions about institutions and inclusion, with preprint and sampling limitations clearly retained.

Cold-chain demand should be studied as a complete service. [GIZ’s fisheries research (PU06)](https://www.giz.de/en/downloads/giz2024-en-studie-somalia.pdf) and the [historical dairy-centre report (PU15)](https://doi.org/10.13140/RG.2.2.22986.77762) show why equipment availability alone is insufficient. The [enterprise survey (PU10)](https://microdata.worldbank.org/catalog/8119) is a separate opportunity for weighted firm analysis, while new sampling is needed for excluded businesses.

Three sources require explicit numerical quarantine, rather than silent correction:

- [BAARIS feasibility report (PU04)](https://baaris.org/storage/media/CiwFfAlLZMRFzbwHlHhgYkzaLpaf1We19QvlhwwV.pdf): incompatible farm-area units and non-additive gender chart entries.
- [Fish-price baseline (PU07)](https://www.mfbe.gov.so/assets/documents/Fish%20Price%20Assessment%20Study%20%28Baseline%29%20Report.pdf): narrative and tabulated female participation disagree.
- [Health-electrification brief (PU08)](https://www.humanitarianenergy.org/assets/resources/HHE-brief-2025.pdf): inconsistent multi-country facility totals and standardized rather than measured load assumptions.

These issues do not erase the sources’ qualitative value. They prevent unqualified use of affected quantities. The [school ESMP (PU09)](https://moewr.gov.so/wp-content/uploads/ESMP-REPORT-Education-Facilities-Northeastern-State-of-Somalia.pdf) presents a different boundary: planned systems require commissioning verification before being counted as operating capacity.

The [Hobyo seaport study (PU18)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-96401-z) also requires a physical feasibility check: its modeled pumped storage does not establish that the needed elevation and reservoir infrastructure exist locally. Its unusually favorable financial outputs remain simulation results.

## Priority data and research opportunities

The following are reviewer recommendations, not claims already demonstrated by the cited studies:

1. **Energy–water operations:** jointly record electricity, flow, head, abstraction, downtime and water quality over wet and dry seasons. Test solar/diesel or hybrid alternatives under safe abstraction limits.
2. **Cold-chain services:** measure daily ice/milk/fish throughput, temperature, ambient conditions, opening hours, equipment efficiency, spoilage and net earnings. Separate ownership, access and benefits for women and other excluded vendors.
3. **Public facilities:** obtain hourly load and critical-service inventories; distinguish oxygen, sterilization, refrigeration, lighting and computing. Track maintenance contracts, battery replacement and actual service interruptions.
4. **Commercial demand:** use enterprise weights and missing-data checks; separately sample informal and smaller businesses. Link actual energy service quality with production, employment and prices.
5. **Intervention evaluation:** compare verified operating systems and appropriate controls, documenting co-interventions, security disruptions and selection. Distinguish reduced fuel expenditure from improved welfare or productivity.

Immediate retrieval leads are the hospital’s original load series, the underlying health and school assessments, corrected feasibility tables, fish-market microdata, and program monitoring files. The search also identified an inaccessible GEEL annual report and an unrecovered original 2016 solar-water evaluation. These are **access gaps**, not evidence that the topics have never been studied. No authors or institutions were contacted, and no model was run.

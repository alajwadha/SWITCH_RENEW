## Judgment

The central recommendation survives review: an empirically grounded, bounded service-area study is a credible initial SWITCH direction if utility collaboration is feasible. The packages distinguish modeled results, observations, policy aspirations and source conflicts unusually clearly. They do not support a calibrated national interconnected model yet. The broad literature review should remain a resource for cooking, transport and productive-use research even if the first SWITCH implementation has a narrower boundary.

The defensible contribution is a decision that improves when evidence improves: reliability and affordability under uncertain demand, fuel, finance and network conditions, with explicit service and distributional outcomes. Merely using SWITCH in place of existing OnSSET/OSeMOSYS/LEAP tools would not establish novelty. The packages already make this distinction; retain it.

I found three actionable reporting/metadata issues below. None demonstrates that the assembled measurements are fabricated or that a model result has been validated. I also identify design improvements separately, so recommendations are not confused with factual errors.

## Optional reviewer observations, distinct from corrections or adopted actions

1. **Use a clear evidence-readiness gate before selecting a baseline.** For each candidate utility/facility, require load provenance and timestamps, operating asset identifiers, power/energy ratings, fuel records, outages, tariff/payment basis and a defined service boundary. Record which criteria are actually available. The existing P1 request list contains most of these ingredients; turn it into a small decision table when selecting the first case.

2. **Require contemporaneous data for operational validation.** Hospital load in PU01 is reported for 2017–2018; E07 wind observations are 2019–2021; the existing hourly POWER sample is 2025. These are useful separate evidence streams but not a synchronous load-weather validation dataset. Ask whether overlapping load and weather years exist. Any resampling or year substitution must be a declared scenario assumption.

3. **Add multiple hourly weather years before making robust adequacy/storage claims.** The 25-year daily history can screen annual/seasonal variability. It cannot recover the timing and duration of all hourly shortages, within-day load/resource correlation or storage cycling. The 2025 hourly year is an initial chronology, not evidence that a design works under all weather conditions. This is a future model-design requirement, not a reason to discard the current resource pack.

4. **Make survey dependence machine-readable.** The synthesis correctly warns that SIHBS/SHDS derivative papers reuse observations. Add stable dataset-family IDs, geography, fieldwork year and sample/weight status to every derivative record, including non-household families such as SEAP and PROSCAL. The 73 count is a publication/source count, not 73 independent empirical replications. Do not rank evidence strength by near-equal thematic publication counts.

5. **Maintain equal research attention through service-specific decisions.** Cooking needs quantities, stacking, refills, expenditure, continued use and measured exposure. Transport needs stock/activity and fuel allocation before attributing benefits to electrification. Productive uses need throughput, temperatures/spoilage, water flow/head/abstraction, critical clinical loads and maintenance. The literature package already recognizes these. The older gap response is primarily an electricity/SWITCH readiness assessment and should be labeled as such when reused as an all-energy gap summary.

6. **Separate research utility from publication quality.** A paper with reporting defects can point to valuable original measurements. Prioritize access to the underlying data and independent checks, rather than adopting a headline simulated LCOE or treating a publication's reputation as input validation. Cross-sectional associations and program interviews do not establish causal energy-service benefits.
